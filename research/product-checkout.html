Agrocom Design Research

Product Detail & Checkout

The winning pattern for agri-commerce is a photo-led product page with a heavyweight seller-trust block, a sticky two-button bar that treats Make Offer as a first-class peer of Buy Now, and a checkout that decides delivery-vs-pickup before it ever asks about payment.

Flow: Product page → Negotiate / Offer → Cart / Direct buy → Checkout (delivery + payment) → Confirmation & tracking  ·  Sources: Mobbin (iOS, deep mode)  ·  2026-08-02

Common patterns

What nearly every strong marketplace product/checkout flow shares.

01Edge-to-edge gallery, dots + counter

Photos run full-bleed to the top of the screen with pagination dots or a "2 of 5" counter (eBay, Mercari, Faire, Taobao). Secondary photos live in a thumbnail strip. Chrome (back, share, cart) floats over the image in circular scrims.

Faire · eBay · Mercari · Amazon · Taobao

02Sticky bottom CTA bar, often dual

The purchase action never scrolls away. C2C marketplaces make it two buttons — Offer + Buy Now (Grailed) or Add to Cart + Buy (Taobao, Hers) — with icon shortcuts (chat, favorite, shop) tucked to the left.

Grailed · Taobao · Faire · Hers

03Seller trust block = numbers, not vibes

Rating + review count, transaction count, response time, and a verified/quick-responder badge, always adjacent to a message button. Faire even shows "50 min" average response next to the 4.9★.

Grailed · Faire · eBay · Etsy

04Offer sheets use preset % chips

Three discount presets (5/10/15% or 10/20/30%) with one marked "Recommended" and pre-selected, plus a custom field over a numeric keypad. The CTA repeats the amount ("Send $61 offer"). Expiry (24h/47h) and "an offer is not a payment" copy set expectations.

Vestiaire · Depop · Vinted · Grailed · Mercari

05Delivery cost & date live on the product page

Shipping fee and estimated delivery window appear under the price before checkout ever starts (eBay's "Free shipping · Est. Fri Feb 20–Thu 26", Mercari's "shipping included", Taobao's policy rows). No cost surprises at the end.

eBay · Mercari · Taobao

06Checkout = fulfillment first, payment last

Order of operations is universal: mode (delivery/pickup toggle) → address/map pin → hand-off instructions → time window → payment → summary → Place Order. CVS literally locks the payment step until shipping is complete.

Uber Eats · Grab · CVS · UNIQLO · Chipotle

07Confirmation is a tracker, not a receipt

Best-in-class apps skip the static "thank you" page: confirmation lands directly on a live status screen — big ETA, stepper (3–5 named states), map, courier contact, and a help entry point.

Chipotle · Glovo · Keeta · Bolt Food · Rappi

08Itemized totals with celebrated savings

Subtotal / delivery fee / service fee / discount rows, with savings called out in color ("Yay, you've saved Rp62.800!"). Fees get explainer tooltips instead of being hidden.

Grab · Chipotle · CVS · UNIQLO

What the best apps do differently

Faire sells in units, not items

Its CTA row is a quantity dropdown showing the extended price ("3 ($9.00)") beside Add to cart — pricing math done for the buyer before commitment. For produce sold per basket, bag, kg, or crate, this is the pattern to steal: unit picker and computed total inside the sticky bar itself.

eBay merges chat and offers into one thread

Instead of separating "message seller" from "make offer," negotiation happens in a chat where offers render as structured cards with amounts and expiry timers, alongside free text. Haggling stays human, but every committed number is machine-readable and one tap from purchase.

Vestiaire coaches the buyer toward acceptable offers

"This item is already priced below average — try our recommendation for your best chance of getting an offer accepted." Guidance plus a highlighted recommended chip converts lowballing into deals that actually close. Mercari goes further and shows the seller's acceptable range outright.

Keeta puts a guarantee inside the tracker

The "On-time Promise" banner (compensation voucher if the order is late) turns the tracking screen from passive status into an active trust contract — exactly the reassurance a first-time farm-goods buyer needs.

Grab treats hand-off logistics as first-class UI

Radio options for "Drop it off / Hand it to me / Meet at doorstep" plus free-text driver instructions acknowledge that addresses alone don't work in many markets — landmark-and-instructions delivery is designed, not improvised. Directly transferable to Nigerian addressing reality.

Recommendations for Agrocom

Negotiation-first commerce for the Nigerian market: haggling via chat + Make Offer, unit pricing, delivery vs pickup, and pay-on-delivery as a visible option.

R1Sticky dual-CTA bar: Make Offer + Buy Now

Nigerian buyers expect to price ("how much last?") before they pay. Follow Grailed: a persistent bottom bar with Make Offer (outlined, green) and Buy ₦25,000 (filled #16A34A), price embedded in the button. Chat icon sits to the left of both. Minimum 56px tall buttons, ClashDisplay numerals — thumb-sure with market gloves on.

R2Offer sheet with ₦ presets + haggle-in-chat continuity

Bottom sheet à la Vestiaire/Depop: three chips (−5% / −10% / −15% off asking, "Recommended" pre-selected), custom ₦ field over a numeric keypad, CTA reading "Send ₦22,500 offer", and copy: "An offer is not a payment. Seller has 24 hours to respond." Every offer and counter-offer then renders as a structured card inside the buyer–seller chat (eBay pattern) with Accept / Counter / Decline — so haggling and checkout share one thread. Always render ₦, never ASCII "N".

R3Unit & quantity pricing in the CTA row

Farm goods sell per paint bucket, bag, basket, kg, crate, tuber. Steal Faire's pattern: a unit/quantity stepper directly in the sticky bar showing the computed total ("2 bags · ₦51,000"), with per-unit price ("₦25,500/50kg bag") shown under the headline price like Amazon/CVS's per-oz line. Bulk-tier hints ("5+ bags: ₦24,000/bag") earn the gold accent.

R4Seller trust block built for agri-trade

Above the fold, Grailed-style: photo, name, verified badge (green check), star rating + count, completed orders, response time ("replies in ~30 min"), and location ("Kaduna · 210 km away") — distance matters for perishables. One-tap Message button beside it. Add harvest date / freshness ("harvested 2 days ago") as an Agrocom-specific trust line.

R5Delivery vs pickup as the first checkout decision

Uber Eats-style segmented toggle at the top of checkout: Delivery / Pickup, with a fee nudge on pickup ("Save ₦1,500 — collect at farm/market stall"). For delivery, follow Grab: landmark-tolerant address form, map pin edit, phone number required, and free-text rider instructions ("Beside the blue kiosk after the junction"). Pickup shows seller's market location, distance, and available hours.

R6Pay-on-delivery as a visible, honest payment tier

Payment methods as chunky UNIQLO-style rows: Pay online (card / transfer / USSD) and Pay on delivery — POD present and unashamed, with an escrow-style reassurance line for online payment ("Money held until you confirm receipt", Keeta's guarantee pattern). Show any POD surcharge inline, never at the final step. Order summary itemizes produce subtotal, delivery fee, and savings from an accepted offer ("You negotiated ₦2,500 off").

R7Confirmation = tracker with big ETA and named steps

Skip the receipt page. Land on a Blinkit/Glovo-style screen: full-bleed green header, huge ETA in ClashDisplay ("Arriving Tue, 10 Aug"), then a 4-step tracker — Order confirmed → Seller packing → On the way → Delivered — as a Bolt Food vertical timeline with timestamps (better for multi-day agri logistics than a same-hour horizontal bar). Courier/seller call + chat buttons, and a "Confirm receipt" action that releases escrow.

Design-language signal

Numbers are the interface. In a haggling marketplace, trust lives in legible figures: the price, the offer, the delivery fee, the ETA. Set every ₦ amount and every step-count in ClashDisplay at display sizes on high-contrast surfaces (dark ink on light cards outdoors; single green #16A34A reserved for the one action that moves money; gold only for savings and freshness). Buttons carry their consequence in the label — "Send ₦22,500 offer", "Buy ₦51,000", "Confirm receipt" — so a farmer in full sun, phone at arm's length, always knows exactly what a tap costs and where the deal stands. That is what keeps chunky, utilitarian commerce feeling trustworthy rather than cheap.
